# Reading Peer Valuation

Company Workbench asks whether a company's valuation multiple is higher or lower than a compatible peer benchmark. The answer is descriptive research context, not a recommendation or an estimate of investment return.

## What the four values mean

- **Company multiple:** price relative to a defined earnings, revenue, cash-flow or enterprise-value measure.
- **Peer median:** the middle multiple among the compatible, reviewed peer observations for that specific measure.
- **Premium / discount:** company multiple divided by the peer median, minus one. A positive value means a higher multiple; a negative value means a lower multiple.
- **Comparable peers:** distinct contributing companies for that measure. Another share class of the same issuer does not add a company. This product requires at least three compatible peers before displaying the comparison.

Illustrative example only: ExampleCo has30× P/E. Three synthetic peers have20×,25× and40×; their median is25×. ExampleCo's multiple is20% above that median. These numbers are not data for a real company.

## Why higher does not automatically mean overvalued

A premium can reflect different expected growth, profitability, business mix, capital needs or risk. Establish whether those differences justify the premium before drawing an investment conclusion. Different metrics can give different comparisons; they are not combined into a company score. [CFA Institute's overview of market-based valuation](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/market-based-valuation-price-enterprise-value-multiples) explains the relationship between multiples, fundamentals and comparable benchmarks.

## When the product withholds a comparison

Missing or unverified prices, definitions, financial periods, source identity, field rights or reviewed peer relationships keep the result withheld. Annual and trailing-twelve-month measures are separate. Reported GAAP earnings and adjusted earnings cannot silently share a comparison. Price dates must match the research evidence cutoff and be within seven calendar days of the review date. The declared financial-period guard allows at most550 days from an annual period end to the price date and180 days for a trailing-twelve-month period; this guard is not proof that a later filing does not exist. Negative or zero denominators do not imply an attractive valuation.

The source disclosure shows contributing companies, definitions, dates and exclusions where evidence exists. A shared industry classification is a candidate-discovery aid, not proof of comparability. Banks, funds, blank-check companies and security wrappers need appropriate research methods and independent input evidence.

The current repository's aggregate peer outputs alone do not provide the new per-metric comparison evidence. Real-company results remain withheld until that source path is established. The labelled example explains the calculation while keeping the selected company's fields unchanged.
